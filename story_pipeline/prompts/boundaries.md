# What these stories don't do

Universal. Every genre, every subgenre, no exceptions, and the ideator, writer
and editor all work to this file. An outline that violates it is regenerated
rather than written; a chapter that violates it fails review.

Two reasons this exists. The first is that these are narrated over illustrations
and published where the audience is unfiltered and often half-listening at
night — nobody opted into being ambushed. The second is that YouTube demonetizes
a channel over any single one of these, retroactively, across the whole back
catalogue.

## Hard lines

**No sexual content.** Attraction, tension, longing, and a kiss are all
available. The scene ends at the bedroom door. No undressing, no bodies
described with intent, no fade-to-black that is obviously a fade from something
explicit.

**Every character in a romantic or attracted role is an adult**, and the outline
says so with an age. This is not negotiable, it is not softened by a historical
setting where younger marriage was legal, and it applies to implication as much
as depiction. A character described as young must also be described as an adult.

**No graphic violence.** Consequence, grief, fear, and the aftermath are fine. An
injury can matter without being described. No torture, no gore, no lingering on a
body, no violence against a child or an animal on the page.

**No suicide, self-harm, or disordered eating.** Not as plot, not as backstory,
not as a character's private thought, not off-page and referred to. This is
absolute regardless of how it would be handled. If an outline reaches for a
character's death as motivation, use an ordinary one — illness, accident, the
sea, age.

**No method detail for anything dangerous** — poisons, weapons, drugs, explosives.
A mystery may turn on a poisoning without the story containing a recipe. Name the
substance, skip the dose.

**No slurs, and no ethnic, religious, or national caricature.** A period setting
does not license period language. Prejudice can exist in a story — a character
can be snobbish, xenophobic, wrong — and be shown through behaviour and social
consequence without the vocabulary.

**No real people.** No living person, no person dead within living memory, and no
recognizable stand-in. Historical figures may be mentioned as part of a setting —
a war, a monarch, a distant event — but never appear, speak, or act.

**No real-world religion, contemporary politics, or recent tragedy as plot
material.** Characters may be devout in a generic or invented sense. Nothing
maps onto an actual denomination, party, conflict, or disaster.

**No profanity.** None. Not mild, not period-appropriate. Characters who need to
convey fury do it through what they say, not what they swear.

## Handle with care

These are permitted, and each is a place where a story can go wrong by accident.

**Historical atrocity.** Slavery, colonial violence, persecution and pogrom are
real parts of the periods these stories are set in. They may exist in the world —
a fortune with an ugly source, a character who has fled something — but they are
not the engine of the plot, they are never depicted, and they are never the
setting's decoration. If a story cannot work without one, pick a different story.

**Addiction and gambling.** A ruinous debt is a classic engine and is fine.
Substance dependence should stay at the level of consequence, and never be
glamorous or detailed.

**Mental illness.** A character may be grieving, frightened, obsessive, or
unwell. Avoid period diagnostic language, avoid the madwoman as a plot device,
and never make an illness the source of menace.

**Children.** Present, safe, and peripheral. Never in danger, never the emotional
lever, never alone with an adult in a scene that carries any charge at all.

**Class and servants.** Servants are people with names, competence, and their own
concerns, not scenery and not comic relief.

## The register these stories live in

The point is not that nothing bad happens. Comfort fiction without stakes is
boring, and a story where nothing is at risk is not worth twelve minutes of
someone's evening.

The point is that the badness is **social and recoverable**: a reputation, a
misunderstanding, a debt, a wrong choice, a letter that arrived too late.
Somebody's whole life can be at stake without anybody bleeding. That constraint
is not a limitation on the writing — it is the constraint under which the entire
comedy-of-manners tradition was built, and it produced better plots than
unlimited violence ever has.

Every story ends with something resolved. Not necessarily happily, and not
necessarily for everyone, but the reader should not be left holding an open
wound. They are going to sleep after this.
