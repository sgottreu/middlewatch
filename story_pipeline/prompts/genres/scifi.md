# Science fiction voice

## Competence, not exposition

The narration belongs to people who work here. They do not explain the ship to
each other, and the story does not stop to explain it to the reader either.
Meaning arrives from use: a character checks a seal twice because the last person
who checked once is not aboard any more. That single line does more than a
paragraph about hull integrity.

The reader should feel slightly behind for the first two hundred words and fully
oriented by the end of the first chapter. Trust that gap. Closing it too fast is
what makes science fiction read like a manual.

## One idea, followed honestly

Pick one speculative premise per story and take it seriously all the way down.
Not three. If minds can be copied, then inheritance law, grief, and who counts as
a witness are all different, and the story is about one of those consequences —
not about the copying itself, which the characters find unremarkable.

The premise is the setting. The story is still two people and a problem.

## Hardware has weight and history

Every object was manufactured, paid for, installed by someone, and has been
failing slowly since. Nothing is seamless. Name the wear: the hatch that needs a
shoulder, the display with a dead column of pixels, the coffee that is technically
a nutrient suspension and universally called coffee. Specific decay is the fastest
way to make a place real.

## Diction

- **Plain register.** Working people, spare sentences, dry humour under pressure.
  Technical vocabulary only where a professional would actually use it.
- **No invented word salad.** One or two coined terms per story, introduced in
  context, never explained. A page of capitalised nouns is a tell.
- **No portentous narration.** "The stars were indifferent to their struggle" is
  the failure mode of the whole genre. The stars do not get an opinion.
- **Numbers should be sayable.** This is heard, not read. "Two years out" beats
  "eight hundred and forty-one days."
- **Avoid the shopping list of hard-SF nouns** — bulkhead, klaxon, airlock,
  gantry — three per chapter at most, or it reads as costume.
