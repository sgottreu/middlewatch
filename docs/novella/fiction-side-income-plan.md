# Fiction Side Income Plan

> Moved from the retired `kindle-novellas` repo on 2026-11-25. Content unchanged.

A rough plan for earning side income from fiction across YouTube, Kindle and audiobooks, while testing which channels and ideas are worth the effort. It applies to any series or standalone novella.

## Goals

- Earn some side income.
- Try different ideas cheaply and put more time into whatever works.

## Workflow

AI writes the first draft and makes the cover art; Scott edits and shapes both into the final book.

### Writing

1. **Story notes:** a one-page brief with the premise, main characters, setting and intended ending.
2. **Outline:** AI turns the brief into chapter beats; rework them until the story works.
3. **Drafting:** AI drafts one chapter at a time, with the brief and outline (or series bible) included every time.
4. **Edit:** rewrite in your own voice, fix inconsistencies and cut generic AI phrasing.
5. **Polish:** final proofread, then prepare the cover and blurb.

### Cover

1. **Study the genre:** look at best-selling covers in the category so the book signals its genre at a glance.
2. **Generate the art:** use an image tool such as Midjourney or ChatGPT for background art only, with no text.
3. **Add the title:** set the title and author name in Canva or a similar design tool, since AI tools struggle with lettering.
4. **Check the size:** about 2560 × 1600 pixels, portrait (confirm current KDP specs); make sure the title reads as a small thumbnail.
5. **Disclose:** KDP's AI-content question covers cover images too.

## Product Line

- **YouTube:** short stories used to build an audience. They may share a setting with the books or stand completely apart.
- **Kindle:** novellas of about 25k to 40k words, either part of a series or standalone. This is the main product.
- **Audiobooks:** narrated versions of the finished novellas, possibly with AI voices to start.

Series with linked standalone books suit all three channels, since readers can start anywhere. Unrelated novellas are fine too, as experiments.

## YouTube Stories That Feed the Novellas

Some YouTube stories share a setting with a novella series, as side tales about other characters or places. Each one ends by pointing to the book in that world. The videos work as free samples and marketing for the books. Audiences may not overlap much at first, but each channel can still send a few people to the others.

## Standalone Novellas

Other novellas have nothing to do with the YouTube channel. They are separate experiments with their own settings, genres or ideas.

- They are sold only on Kindle and as audiobooks, without YouTube support.
- They show whether a book can sell on its own, through Kindle search, categories and keywords.
- They can go into Kindle Unlimited freely, since nothing from them appears elsewhere.
- A standalone that does well can later grow into a series, or get its own YouTube side tales.

## Release Pace

- A YouTube video every week or two.
- A novella every two or three months.
- A steady pace matters more than speed, especially on Kindle.

## Costs

Mostly time, plus covers and narration. Start cheap and only spend more once a channel shows results.

## Exclusivity and Rights

- If an ebook is enrolled in Kindle Unlimited (KDP Select), its content must be exclusive to Amazon while enrolled. It can't be published elsewhere, including free on YouTube.
- So keep YouTube stories as separate side tales, not excerpts or chapters from the novellas.
  - Kindle Unlimited exclusivity covers only the ebook. An audio version of a Kindle book can likely go on YouTube without breaking it.
  - If an audiobook is sold through Audible on an exclusive deal, posting the full audio free on YouTube would likely break that deal.
- Amazon KDP asks authors to disclose AI-generated content when publishing. Answer honestly, even when the AI draft has been heavily edited.
- Check the current KDP terms before publishing, since rules can change.

## Testing and Success Signals

Decide on a simple signal and timeframe for each channel before starting, then compare results.

- **YouTube:** views and subscriber growth after about ten videos.
- **Kindle:** sales and page reads in the first month or two after the first novella.
- **Audiobooks:** sales after release, tried only once a novella has done reasonably well.

Put more effort into whichever channel catches on, and scale back or change approach on the ones that don't.

## First Steps

1. Choose the first project: a series setting or a standalone novella.
2. Outline the first novella.
3. Write two or three short stories for YouTube.
4. Set the exact success signals and dates for each test.
