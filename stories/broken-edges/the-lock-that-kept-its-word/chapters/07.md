## Chapter 7. The Price

The strongbox sat dripping on the boards of Maud Brennick's boat, with the harbour flat and bright on every side of it. Walter Ames knelt in front of it and laid the oilcloth letter by his knee.

**Jory Brennick** “You don't have to be the one.”

**Walter Ames** “She wrote that she'd not ask another to pay it. I'd look poorly beside her if I did.”

He set his right hand flat on the smooth plate.

**Walter Ames** “Ames.”

The word went out across the water and the water did nothing with it. A gull came over the mast line and kept going. Liora Quill hung at the gunwale with her elbows on the boards and did not climb in.

**Walter Ames** “Ames.”

Jory Brennick saw the plate go darker. Not wet-dark. The other way, as a coal goes when the fire has finished with it.

**Walter Ames** “Ames.”

Then the cold came up out of it. The skin of Walter's hand went white in a line across the knuckles, and the line climbed past the wrist and under the cuff. He did not take his hand away. Something heavy turned over inside the box, and the lid rose half an inch with a click like a dropped pebble.

Walter sat back on his heels and held the hand against his chest. The tips of his fingers were the colour of candle wax. He opened and closed them, and they came back slowly, and not all the way.

**Maud Brennick** “Let me see that hand.”

**Walter Ames** “In a moment. It's open.”

He raised the lid with his left hand. There were no bottles in it, and nothing already measured out. There was a book bound in leather gone black, with a brown tide-mark across the lower third of every page. Beside it lay a lump the size of a fist, wrapped in oiled sailcloth and tied with cord.

**Jory Brennick** “That's not medicine.”

**Walter Ames** “No. That's better. That's how.”

Jory worked the cord off with his thumbnail. The stone inside was grey and dull as a street cobble until he turned it, and then a green came up through it and went out again, like a fish under a hull.

**Liora Quill** “Put it down.”

**Jory Brennick** “I've held worse.”

Maud took it off him anyway and held it in the sailcloth. Walter had not looked up from the book. He set his thumb flat on the first page, where the ink had bloomed at the fold, and kept it there.

**Jory Brennick** “Mr Ames. For my book, so it's written right. Whose cellar was that, and whose box?”

Walter opened his mouth. His breath came out. He tried again, and the sound that should have been his name was not in him, the way a step is not there in the dark. He touched his own throat.

**Walter Ames** “Walter. I can say Walter. I can say my grandmother. I cannot say the other.”

**Liora Quill** “Try it slow.”

**Walter Ames** “I have. Twice.”

He looked at the smooth dark plate.

**Walter Ames** “That was the price. She paid it to shut the thing and I've paid it to open it. I don't suppose she knew it would be the name itself.”

Maud was still turning the wrapped stone an inch at a time in the sailcloth, and her face had changed.

**Maud Brennick** “Jory. I've had three of these through my hands since the spring. Off the bottom, out past the dyers' yard, where the harbour floor cracked open. Boys bring them in and nobody will buy them.”

**Jory Brennick** “Same stone?”

**Maud Brennick** “Same stone. Which means she wrote her measures against the very stuff we're still pulling up. Twenty years dead, and she's the only hand that ever wrote them.”
