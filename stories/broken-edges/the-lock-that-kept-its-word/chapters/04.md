## Chapter 4. The Grandmother's Letter

Walter Ames kept his shop two streets above the place where the Hartsford hill gave out and the water began. It smelled of bay and dust and something sharp underneath. He swept a row of herb bundles off the counter to make room for four pairs of elbows, and he listened while Jory Brennick told it in order: the long back room, the bench of cracked glass, the stone mortar the size of a wash basin, the strongbox bolted at chest height to an oak post.

**Jory Brennick** “That's no cellar. Nobody grinds in their cellar with a mortar you'd need two men to lift.”

**Walter Ames** “No. No, they don't.”

**Jory Brennick** “And the wall by the post's been chiselled. Five bites out of it, square-edged, grey as a fresh break. Everything else down there has twenty years of weed on it. Those have nothing.”

Walter's hand had gone up to the loupe at his throat without his noticing it.

**Jory Brennick** “The lock has no keyhole. There's letters standing proud off the plate, spaced like a name. And it was warm, Mr Ames. Warm through a wet glove, in water that aches your jaw.”

Walter went into the back and came out with the oilcloth. He unfolded the letter on the counter and turned past the first page, which he had plainly read a hundred times, to the second, which he had not.

**Walter Ames** “She writes about the fishers bringing things up off the bottom that autumn. There was a crack opened in the seabed when the land went down, and pieces of it came up in the nets. She calls it the stone that bleeds light.”

**Maud Brennick** “They still come up.”

**Walter Ames** “She ground it. That's what the mortar's for. There was a fever in the camps that first winter, up where people were putting roofs on the hill, and she lost a good many of them and could do nothing. So she tried what nobody had tried. She says here she wrote down every measure, and burned the first three sets of measures as useless.”

**Liora Quill** “And the fourth?”

**Walter Ames** “The fourth she kept.”

He read on with his finger under the line, and his voice got slower rather than quieter.

**Walter Ames** “She sealed her notes and a sample of the stone in the box, and she made the lock out of the same stone. She says it answers to the household's true name, spoken three times, in open air. Never under the water. She is firm about that twice over.”

**Jory Brennick** “Then the box has to come up before anyone says a word.”

**Walter Ames** “There's more. She writes, it will have its price of whoever speaks. I could not learn the price beforehand, and I would not ask another to pay it in my place.”

Nobody said anything to that for a moment. Maud Brennick folded her arms and looked at the shelf of jars behind Walter's head.

**Maud Brennick** “That's an honest sentence. I'd rather it weren't.”

The shop bell went. Selah Vance ducked under the lintel with her coat still dripping from the walk up and her cap in her hand.

**Selah Vance** “They told me at the landing you'd all come up here. I came to hear whether you'd got into it.”

**Jory Brennick** “We're in. Through a gap in the wall the tremor opened. There's cut marks beside it, fresh ones, and none of us can account for them.”

**Selah Vance** “I can. That'll be my survey crew. Two of them went down last week with a bar to set marks for the piling line, and the wall there is the only stone standing straight enough to cut into. Three days before the quarter moon. I've got it in the book if you want to see it.”

Liora Quill let out a breath and started laughing, and stopped herself.

**Selah Vance** “That's the good half of what I came to say. The weather past the point has turned faster than the harbour men called it. I can't drive in a sea and I'll not have the frame out there when it hits.”

She put her cap back on.

**Selah Vance** “Pilings go in four days. Not six.”
