## Chapter 3. Through the Broken Wall

The gap in the Kettle Row wall was a shoulder wide and no more, and it took Jory Brennick two tries to get through it. He went in with both arms ahead of him, stone dragging at his ribs. Then he was through, into water so black he had to hold still until his eyes found the green smear of daylight behind him.

Liora Quill came after, smaller and smarter about it, and turned a slow circle beside him with her hand out.

It was a back room, a long one. Silt stood up off the floor where their feet went near it, so they swam high, under a ceiling that had sagged but held. The far wall had a bench along the whole length of it.

Glass. Rows of it, racked and fallen and cracked across, bellied vessels with long necks, flat dishes, a jar with its stopper still seated. Between them lay iron gone to orange lumps that had been tools once. A stone mortar sat at the end of the bench. It was the size of a wash basin, and it had not moved since the water came. Nobody kept that in a house cellar.

Liora found the post first. It ran floor to ceiling, a squared oak beam holding the sag out of the boards above, and bolted to it at chest height was a strongbox the size of a bread crock.

Jory got close before he saw the wall beside it. The stone there was scarred, four or five short bites of a chisel, the edges square, the colour of fresh-broken grey. Everything else wore twenty years of weed and soft brown fur. These cuts wore nothing at all.

He put his glove on the lid and worked round to the lock plate. There was no keyhole. There was no slot, no hasp, no shackle. Only a plate of something darker than iron, with letters standing proud of it, spaced out like a name said slowly.

And it was warm. Through a wet glove, in water cold enough to ache in the jaw, it was warm, the way the back of a dog is warm.

Jory took his hand away. His chest had started to pull. Liora was already at his elbow with two fingers raised, pointing up.

* * *

A minute later they came up into the morning glare off the harbour, ten feet apart, and Maud Brennick had the boat between them before either had finished breathing.

**Liora Quill** “Somebody's been cutting at that wall.”

**Jory Brennick** “Four marks. Five. Right alongside the box, and clean as a quarry face.”

**Maud Brennick** “How clean?”

**Jory Brennick** “No growth on them. Everything else down there has a beard.”

Maud sat back on the thwart and looked at the water as if it owed her an explanation.

**Maud Brennick** “Weed takes hold inside a season on bare stone. I've watched it do it. Anything cut before midsummer would be furred by now.”

**Liora Quill** “So it was cut after midsummer.”

**Jory Brennick** “By who? The old man's only had the letter a month and he can't swim a stroke. Nobody dives Kettle Row.”

**Liora Quill** “There's a box bolted to a post in it.”

Jory hauled himself over the side and sat dripping with his arms on his knees. He could still feel the warmth of that plate in the palm of his glove.

**Jory Brennick** “And a lock with no keyhole. Letters on it. A name, I think.”

Maud looked at him then, and whatever she had been going to say about weed, she let it go.

**Maud Brennick** “Then we're not asking me. Row us in. Your herbalist has a letter he's only read the front page of, and I want to hear the rest of it out loud.”
