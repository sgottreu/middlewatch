## Chapter 4. The Road

Garret, Elin, and Dessa were out of Earlmere on three horses before the mist had come off the millpond. Elin's father had handed over his plough mare and gone back inside, latching the door behind him. Dessa rode in front, and at the fork above the east cut she took them off the cart road.

**Garret Marsh** “That's not the Tollgate road.”

**Dessa Fenwick** “No. A loaded cart cannot take this shoulder. We can. It comes out at the ford a good half day short of them.”

The track climbed hard for an hour. Elin kept one hand on the waxed pouch at her hip, where a single leaf off Jasper Lund's rack lay flat between two squares of card.

**Elin Hargrove** “Say we catch them. What then?”

**Garret Marsh** “Then I tell the buyer what he's bought.”

**Elin Hargrove** “You scrape a leaf with your thumbnail and smell it. I had to take your word for it. A Tollgate buyer with forty cords paid for will take the word of the man holding the money.”

Garret said nothing for a while. The mare picked her way over loose stone.

**Garret Marsh** “And they'll say the old cutter from Earlmere is talking down another man's crop to lift the price of his own.”

At the stream below the ridge they got down to water the horses. Elin crouched on the bank, and stopped with her hands in the cold.

**Elin Hargrove** “Last winter you had me sorting in the shed with no brazier. My fingers went dead and I breathed on them, and on the leaves as well, to see the veins through the frost.”

**Garret Marsh** “I remember you complaining of it.”

**Elin Hargrove** “The good ones went warm. Only a little, like a stone that's been in a pocket. The rejects stayed cold as a window latch and the fog sat on them. I thought everyone knew.”

**Garret Marsh** “Nobody knows it. Nobody has ever put it to the test.”

He took a leaf from his own cord and gave it to her. Elin held it to her mouth and breathed. The mist thinned off the glass before she had counted three. The leaf was warm against her lip. She set it down and took Jasper's leaf out of the pouch.

The fog sat on it. She counted to ten and it was still sitting there. The leaf stayed as cold as the stream water.

**Elin Hargrove** “Cold. It's still cold.”

**Dessa Fenwick** “Do it again and let me see.”

She did it again. Dessa watched, then put her hat back on and went for the horses.

* * *

They came down to the ford below Tollgate in the middle of the afternoon. A man in a buyer's grey coat was counting bundles off the tail of the cart into a crate, two at a time, while the driver held the tally board. Garret put his heels in. Elin brought the mare up level with him.

**Elin Hargrove** “Don't stop him with your word. Stop him with mine. Let me breathe on one in front of the lot of them.”
