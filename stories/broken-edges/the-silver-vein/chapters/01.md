## Chapter 1. Silver Veins

The drying shed behind the Hargrove mill smelled of hot wire and candle wax. Garret Marsh lifted a glass leaf off the rack with two fingers and held it up against the lamp so Elin could see the vein running down its middle.

**Garret Marsh** “Tell me what you've got there.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Silver. Stem to tip, no break in it.”

**Garret Marsh** “Then whoever cut it cut it right.”

He laid it in the counting tray. The rule was older than the trade it fed, and it was only one sentence long. A glass leaf is cut when the centre vein has gone silver the whole way, and never a day sooner. Cut it green and it still looks like a leaf. It still hangs on a cord at a traveller's saddle. It simply never sounds.

Elin reached for the next one along, where the silver stopped two thirds down and went to grey. Garret caught her wrist before her fingers closed.

**Garret Marsh** “Not that one.”

**Elin Hargrove** “It's nearly there. Another week of hanging and nobody could tell the difference.”

**Garret Marsh** “Nearly is no good to a man in a hollow of bad air. Twenty-two years I've cut in this wood, and the rule has not failed me once. Not one time. An early leaf lies to you quietly, and you only find out the once.”

He let go of her wrist and went back to sorting. Elin rubbed the place where his glove had gripped and said nothing, which she thought was a considerable act of discipline.

Then boots came fast across the yard, and a woman put her shoulder against the shed door without knocking. Her hat was still tied under her chin and her hands were shaking.

**Dessa Fenwick** “Which of you two knows these?”

She dropped a bundle on the bench. Six leaves strung on a cord, travel-stained, with a wooden tag at the knot. Garret put a hand flat on it.

**Dessa Fenwick** “Dessa Fenwick. I run Ashfall to Tollgate. Day before yesterday my mule stopped at the lip of a hollow and would not go down, and I kicked her on, because these were on my saddle and these were quiet. Three steps in and the ground tipped. She hauled me back up the slope by my own reins.”

**Garret Marsh** “And they never sounded.”

**Dessa Fenwick** “Not a whisper. I want to be told it was my own carelessness. I'd honestly rather hear it.”

Garret shook the bundle beside his ear. Nothing came off it at all. He turned the tag over to the burned mark on its back.

**Garret Marsh** “Odell's mark. Batch eleven, this autumn's cut.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Then they're ours. Somebody in Earlmere stood in a shed like this one and strung them.”

Garret read the number twice and set the tag down very carefully, as though it might go off.

**Garret Marsh** “Every harvester on this side of the wood cuts under Mabel or under me, and I know every hand of them by their shears. Nobody I know cut these.”
