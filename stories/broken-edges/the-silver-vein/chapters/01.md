## Chapter 1. Silver Veins

Behind the Hargrove mill the drying racks stood out in the open yard, under a slant roof with no walls, so the wind could get at the leaves. Garret Marsh lifted one off its cord with two fingers and held it up to the light for Elin to see the vein running down the middle of it.

**Garret Marsh** “Tell me what you've got there.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Silver. Stem to tip, no break in it.”

**Garret Marsh** “Then whoever cut it cut it right.”

He laid it in the counting tray. The rule was older than the trade it fed, and it ran to a single sentence. A glass leaf is cut when the centre vein has gone silver the whole way, and never a day sooner. Cut green, it still looks like a leaf. It still hangs on a cord at a traveller's saddle. It simply never sounds.

Elin reached for the next one along, where the silver stopped two thirds down and went grey. Garret put his palm over the back of her hand and pushed it down to the bench.

**Garret Marsh** “Not that one.”

**Elin Hargrove** “It's nearly there. Another week hanging and nobody could tell.”

**Garret Marsh** “Nearly is no good to a man standing in a hollow of bad air. Twenty-two years I've cut in this wood, and the rule has not failed me once. An early leaf lies to you quietly. You find out the one time you needed it.”

He lifted his hand away and went back to sorting. Elin left hers where he had put it and said nothing, which she thought a considerable act of discipline.

A mule came up the lane at a walk and stopped at the gate. The woman leading it did not tie it off. She crossed the yard with her hat still knotted under her chin and a bundle of leaves in her fist.

**Dessa Fenwick** “Which of you knows these?”

She put them down on the sorting bench. Six leaves strung on a cord, travel-stained, with a wooden tag at the knot.

**Dessa Fenwick** “Dessa Fenwick. I run Ashfall to Tollgate. Day before yesterday my mule stopped at the lip of a hollow and would not go down. I kicked her on, because these were hanging off my saddle and these were quiet. Three steps in and the ground tipped under me. She hauled me back up the slope by my own reins.”

**Garret Marsh** “And they never sounded.”

**Dessa Fenwick** “Not a whisper. I'd like to be told it was my own carelessness. I'd honestly rather hear that.”

Garret shook the bundle beside his ear. Nothing came off it at all. He turned the tag over to the mark burned into the back of it.

**Garret Marsh** “Odell's mark. Batch eleven, this autumn's cut.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Who cut eleven?”

Garret read the number twice and set the tag down carefully, as though it might go off.

**Garret Marsh** “Every harvester this side of the wood cuts under Mabel Odell or under me, and I know each one of them by their shears. Nobody I know cut these.”
