## Chapter 2. The Ledger

Mabel Odell's trading post stood at the top of Earlmere's one street, and she had the shutters down and her chalk out before Garret Marsh was through the door. He put the dead bundle on her counter, between the coin box and a dish of sealing wax. Elin came in behind him and shut the door against the wind.

**Garret Marsh** “Batch eleven. Your mark on the tag. I want to see it in your book.”

**Mabel Odell** “You want to see it, or you want to tell me my book is wrong?”

She fetched the ledger down all the same and turned it to face them. Nine names in the autumn column, each with a burned mark copied beside it in her own hand.

**Mabel Odell** “Nine harvesters cutting for me, and every one of them sworn in this room with my hand on their shears. Silver the whole way or they bring me nothing. I have never taken a green leaf across this counter in eleven years, Garret, and I have never once been short on a tally.”

**Garret Marsh** “Somebody's leaves went quiet over a hollow of bad air in the Marches, and a trader walked out of it by luck. Luck, Mabel.”

Elin had not looked up from the page. She had kept her father's mill accounts since she was fourteen, and a column of weights read to her the way the wood read to Garret.

**Elin Hargrove** “Who is Jasper Lund?”

**Mabel Odell** “Lund's grove is out past the east cut. Why?”

**Elin Hargrove** “Because he's brought you eleven deliveries since the start of the month and every one of them full weight. The other eight are down to half what they fetched in spring. My father says the mature trees out east were cut over before I was born.”

Mabel put the chalk down. For a moment the only sound was the fire.

**Mabel Odell** “He's a good cutter. He was taught right.”

**Garret Marsh** “That is not an answer to the weights.”

**Mabel Odell** “His mother has been abed since the spring. The apothecary at Tollgate sends the draught up with the carters and he wants paying in coin, not in fish or favours. Jasper has the grove to keep as well. I knew the sums were good. I did not ask him how.”

**Garret Marsh** “No. You didn't.”

**Mabel Odell** “Half this town eats off what I send to Tollgate, and you can look at me like that in your own time.”

She shut the ledger and held her hand flat on the cover a moment before she spoke again.

**Mabel Odell** “Go and see his drying racks. Both of you, and I'll say I sent you. But you go tonight.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Why tonight?”

**Mabel Odell** “Because the next load goes down to Tollgate at first light, and his bundles are already on the cart.”
