## Chapter 2. The Ledger

Mabel Odell's trading post stood at the top of Earlmere's one street, and she had the shutters down and her chalk out before Garret Marsh was through the door. He put the dead bundle on her counter, between the coin box and a dish of sealing wax. Elin came in behind him and shut the door against the wind.

**Garret Marsh** “Mabel. Your mark's on that tag, batch eleven. I'd take it kindly if you'd open your book for me.”

**Mabel Odell** “You've been talking to the Fenwick woman, then. She came past here with her hat still on.”

She fetched the ledger down and turned it to face them herself. Nine names in the autumn column, each with a burned mark copied beside it in her own hand.

**Mabel Odell** “Nine harvesters cutting for me, and every one sworn in this room with my hand on their shears. Silver the whole way or they bring me nothing. Eleven years I've kept this book, Garret, and I've never once been short on a tally.”

**Garret Marsh** “I know it. I'm not here to tell you your sums are wrong. I'm here because a bundle with your mark hung quiet over a hollow of bad air in the Marches, and the woman carrying it got out by her mule's good sense.”

**Mabel Odell** “Then we'd both like to know how.”

Elin had not looked up from the page. She had kept her father's mill accounts since she was fourteen, and a column of weights read to her the way the wood read to Garret.

**Elin Hargrove** “Who is Jasper Lund?”

**Mabel Odell** “Lund's grove is out past the east cut. Why?”

**Elin Hargrove** “Because he's brought you eleven deliveries since the start of the month and every one of them full weight. The other eight are down to half what they fetched in spring. My father says the mature trees out east were cut over before I was born.”

Mabel put the chalk down. For a moment the only sound was the fire.

**Mabel Odell** “He's a good cutter. He was taught right.”

**Garret Marsh** “I'd say the same of him. But the weights, Mabel.”

**Mabel Odell** “His mother has been abed since the spring. The apothecary at Tollgate sends the draught up with the carters and he wants paying in coin, not in fish or favours. Jasper has the grove to keep as well.”

She looked at the column a while longer before she said the rest of it.

**Mabel Odell** “I saw the sums were good. I was glad of them, and I didn't ask him how.”

**Garret Marsh** “There's not a broker in the wood who'd have asked. I'd sooner we found out ourselves than have Tollgate find out for us.”

**Mabel Odell** “If word gets down there that a leaf out of Earlmere stayed quiet, there'll be no trade left for either of us to argue over.”

She shut the ledger and held her hand flat on the cover a moment before she spoke again.

**Mabel Odell** “Go and see his drying racks. Both of you, and say I sent you. But you go tonight.”

**Elin Hargrove** “Why tonight?”

**Mabel Odell** “Because the next load goes down to Tollgate at first light, and his bundles are already on the cart.”
