## Chapter 3. The Dye

Jasper Lund's homestead stood an hour east of Earlmere, on slopes that were mostly stumps and whip-thin saplings. His drying shed had no door, only a grain sack hung on a nail. Elin Hargrove went in ahead with the lantern.

The racks were full. Forty cords of leaf at least, every centre vein bright as a new coin from knot to tip.

**Elin Hargrove** “They're all perfect. Every single one.”

**Garret Marsh** “Nothing in this wood is all of anything.”

Elin turned a leaf toward the light, the way he had taught her. Then she rubbed it, which he had not taught her at all. She held her thumb out flat.

**Elin Hargrove** “It came off on me.”

Garret pulled his gauntlet off with his teeth and took the leaf. He scraped it once with a thumbnail and held the scraping under his nose.

**Garret Marsh** “Whortle-berry and ash water. The fullers use it to dye wool grey. Under the paint this vein stops a third of the way down and goes the colour of a dead moth.”

The sack across the doorway lifted. Jasper Lund stood in it with a bucket in one hand. He looked at the lantern, then at the rack above their heads, and set the bucket down on the step.

**Jasper Lund** “The brush is in the crock by the step. You'll want that too.”

**Garret Marsh** “How long?”

**Jasper Lund** “Since the second week of the month.” “There isn't a tree old enough to cut between this fence and the river. My grandfather took them, then my father, then me. I walk four hours out and come back with nine good leaves.”

**Garret Marsh** “Then you bring Mabel nine.”

**Jasper Lund** “Nine doesn't pay a carter. The draught for my mother comes up from Tollgate every ten days and the man wants coin in his hand at the door. Not fish. Not favours.”

**Elin Hargrove** “People hang these on a saddle and ride into the Marches with them.” said Elin.

**Jasper Lund** “I know what they hang them on.”

He wiped his stained palm down his tunic, which did nothing to either.

**Jasper Lund** “I told myself the bad hollows are marked on every map a trader carries.”

**Garret Marsh** “Dessa Fenwick's mule dragged her back up a slope by her own reins. She came to us to be told it was her own fault. I'd have you say that to her face.”

* * *

It was full light on the track outside when a man from the holding below came up at a run, hatless. Mabel's cart had not waited for first light. It had gone at the turn of the night, because Kettering's buyer caravan was at the ford and would not hold past morning. Jasper's bundles went with it.

**Elin Hargrove** “How far ahead is that?”

**Garret Marsh** “By the time we have horses under us, a full day. And the buyer pays on sight.”

He was already walking for the gate.

**Garret Marsh** “Fetch your father's horse and fetch the trader. She knows that road better than the fool driving it.”
