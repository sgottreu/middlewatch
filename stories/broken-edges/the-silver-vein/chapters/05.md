## Chapter 5. The Test

Garret Marsh put his horse across the tail of the cart before the buyer in the grey coat could lay the next two bundles in the crate.

**Garret Marsh** “Stop your counting. Hear the girl, then count.”

Elin Hargrove got down with a leaf in each hand.

**Elin Hargrove** “This one is off my master's cord, cut this autumn. Watch the glass, not me.”

She breathed on it. The mist came up grey across the vein and thinned away while she held it out at arm's length. Then she took Jasper Lund's leaf and breathed on that.

The fog sat on it. She counted aloud to ten and it was still sitting there, over a vein as bright as a new coin.

**Elin Hargrove** “Now rub it.”

The buyer rubbed it, looked at the silver on his thumb, and wiped his hand down his coat.

**Garret Marsh** “Whortle-berry and ash water. Forty cords of it.”

The buyer stepped back from the crate and told the driver to load the whole lot again.

**Dessa Fenwick** “Whose mark is burned on every tag in that crate?”

The driver did not need to read it. "Odell's mark," he said, "so Odell stands the loss, down to the last cord, and she can hear it from me." The bundles went back up the hill unsold, and the loss came off her credit in Tollgate.

* * *

In Earlmere, four days later, half the street stood at Mabel Odell's counter wanting Jasper Lund's grove sold to cover what the town had lost. Garret waited by the fire until they had finished.

**Garret Marsh** “Sell the grove and you've a man with nothing, and nothing is what paints a leaf. He cuts under me. Every bundle to my shed, weighed and passed by my hand. Three seasons, and the debt comes off in leaf.”

**Jasper Lund** “And the draught from Tollgate? It comes every ten days.”

**Mabel Odell** “Advanced against your cut and written in my book where anybody may read it. Poor wages, and your mother has her medicine.”

Jasper said he would take it. Nobody shook his hand.

* * *

At the end of the month the leaf book was settled for the autumn in the old sawmill office. Six of them sat round the table, everybody in Earlmere who cuts glass or writes down what is cut. Garret put the two leaves in front of them, the warm one and the cold one.

**Garret Marsh** “Write it in. Call it the breath test. A ripe leaf takes the mist off itself in a count of three. A green one holds it, dye or no dye. We've run it forty times and it has not missed.”

The woman with the pen asked whose finding it was, her nib already down on the page.

**Garret Marsh** “Elin Hargrove's. Nineteen years old, miller's daughter, and my apprentice as of this morning if she'll have the work.”

Elin put both hands flat on the table and looked at the two leaves.

**Garret Marsh** “Twenty-two years I cut in that wood and never once thought to breathe on one. Go on. You write it down. It's your hand it belongs in.”
