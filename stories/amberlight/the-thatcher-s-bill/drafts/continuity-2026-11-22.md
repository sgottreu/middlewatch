# Continuity check against The Cracked Beam — 2026-11-22

Checked the outline (autumn 1815) against the **finished chapter text** of The
Cracked Beam, not just the bible. Ask the ideator, reading only the bible, said
The Cracked Beam "is silent on the specific texture of their estrangement". It
is not.

## What The Cracked Beam establishes

- Rowe's dislike begins in **January 1817**: Judith corrects his misquoted text
  in the library in front of customers (ch. 2). In ch. 6 he tells Owen: "I have
  known your sister four years and disliked her for one of them, and on Friday
  I found that I had built the dislike myself out of one morning in January."
  In ch. 7, "five months" of going back to that half minute.
- Judith's grievance is **February 1817**: he waves away her list of novels for
  Nan Corbin as "circulating trash" (ch. 2).
- On the Bramdean road (ch. 4) the two name exactly those grievances and find
  both "had grown larger with telling". That is the whole rift as the produced
  story tells it.

## Where this outline collides

1. **The ending.** Ch. 5 leaves them "each still certain of the other's pride"
   with a distant bow, in autumn 1815: a settled mutual judgment more than a
   year before the January morning Rowe says he built his dislike from. The real
   conflict, and audible to anyone who has heard The Cracked Beam.
2. **Judith's trigger.** Ch. 1 has her judge him proud from "a curt note Rowe
   once sent her returning a borrowed book" — a second origin for her side,
   when The Cracked Beam gives it as the February novels. Softer, but avoidable.
3. **The thatch.** The old schoolroom is re-thatched in summer 1815, yet in 1817
   the sexton says the damp "had likely been getting in since before the old
   rector died", above the north gable. Survivable (gable, not thatch), but the
   repair does no work for the later story.

## Suggested fix

Keep the secret payment and Judith's private disappointment; take the
*settled* misjudgment out of Rowe.

- Ch. 5: cut the last two beats (the curt note confirming her opinion; the
  mutual-pride bow). End with the truth held by the viewer and Judith alone,
  and the two of them still only acquaintances, with no fixed opinion on his
  side.
- Ch. 1: replace the "curt note" trigger with the parish's gossip about the
  debt, so her first real grievance stays February 1817.
- Optional: make the 1815 repair something other than the schoolroom roof, or
  leave it.

This reverses part of the 2026-11-11 revision notes ("each think the other
proud, and that misjudgment must still be standing on the last page"), which
were written before the conflict was found. Untick "the chapters and their
beats" to apply it, and check it first with Ask the ideator with "read this
