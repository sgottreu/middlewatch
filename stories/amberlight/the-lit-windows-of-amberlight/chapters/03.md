## Chapter 3. An Account Settled

By Saturday Mrs Pike had the whole of it, and the whole of it now had a chained door in it.

**Mrs Pike** “Chained, my dear, on the inside, at the top of the stairs. A door is not chained against a person who may be spoken to. I do not say it unkindly. I say only that there is a reason the shutters have not come off that front, and it is not the damp.”

She said this across the draper's counter to four people, of whom Mrs Hale was one. Mrs Hale pushed her spectacles up into her hair and gave her own portion exactly as she had received it.

**Mrs Hale** “The Ludd boy and the second Barrow girl both had it on Thursday. They were doing their letters in the east wing, and they put their ears to the wall by the stove, and they heard a lady crying the other side of it. Not calling out. Crying. The Barrow girl said it went on through two whole readings and then stopped.”

**Mrs Pike** “There. Through a wall.”

Phoebe Langton had come in to settle her running account for thread and trim, which stood at one pound eleven and had stood at it since Michaelmas. She put her money on the counter. Mrs Hale did not take it up.

**Mrs Hale** “It is paid, my dear. Paid Friday, in coin, by a gentleman's man I did not know. He asked what Miss Langton owed, he counted it out, and he would not give a name to put against it. I wrote you a receipt because I will not have money in this shop with nothing written against it.”

She found the receipt under the till and handed it over. Phoebe read the line at the foot of it, where the man had written the sum in words at Mrs Hale's asking.

It was a short hand, upright, with the figures crowded close and the letters leaning back from them. She had the same hand folded in her workbox at home, six lines of it, ordering full mourning within the week for a Miss Fairfax.

**Mrs Pike** “Well. Somebody has been generous.”

**Miss Langton** “Somebody has been generous twice, and has not once had the civility to put his name to it.”

She took up her own money again, and the three guineas with it, and went out before Mrs Pike could ask her where.

* * *

It was half past three when Phoebe came up the lane to Amberlight House, and already going grey over the yard wall. She had the coins in her fist and the plain speech ready: that she had been paid off once and paid for twice, and would be obliged if Mr Harker would take back the whole of it and deal with her as he would with any man who sold him cloth.

The front door stood open. Not ajar. Open, wide, with the cold going straight into the hall and nobody on the step.

From somewhere above her came a sound she could not mistake, a fall, heavy and short, and then the ring of something rolling on bare boards. A man's voice called out one name, twice, and there was no coldness in it at all.

**Mr Harker** “Augusta.”
