## Chapter 2. The Measuring

Phoebe Langton went up the lane to Amberlight House on the Thursday, with her measuring tape at her wrist and the black crape under her arm, and was kept four minutes on the step before the door was opened.

The front hall had no fire in it. No candles were lit either, at eleven in the morning. The shutters on that side were still across, and all the light came down the staircase from somewhere above. A tall man in a plain dark coat came out of the room on the left and did not ask her to sit.

**Mr Harker** “You are the dressmaker. Harker. The gown is to be black, to the throat, no trimming of any kind.”

**Miss Langton** “Then I will take Miss Fairfax's measure, sir, if she is at liberty.”

He went back into the room and came out with a gown folded over his arm, grey poplin, worn thin at the elbow.

**Mr Harker** “Take them from this. It fits her within a little.”

**Miss Langton** “Within a little is not a fit, sir. I have made for ladies these seven years and never yet cut to a gown lying in my arms instead of standing up in it.”

**Mr Harker** “You will manage.”

Phoebe took the poplin. Then she set it on the hall chair and faced him.

**Miss Langton** “A tradeswoman is owed the same civility as any customer who walks into the draper's below. The civility I ask is to see the lady I am to sew for.”

Harker looked past her at the shut front door.

**Mr Harker** “Your business is the cloth. It is not my household.”

He turned to take up the gown himself, and in turning he left the door behind him standing a hand's breadth open.

Phoebe saw a window seat with the grey morning on it. A young lady in black sat there, a packet of letters loose in her lap. She was crying without any noise at all, in the way of a person who has been at it some while and expects to be at it longer. She was nineteen, perhaps, and no more raving than the chair she sat on.

Harker saw where Phoebe was looking. He crossed and shut the door.

**Mr Harker** “The order is cancelled. You will not come again.”

**Miss Langton** “Nothing is cut. There is nothing owing.”

He put the coins into her hand and closed her fingers over them. Then the door was open behind her and she was on the step, with the cold coming off the shut rooms at her back.

She stood in the lane and counted it. Three guineas, for a gown nobody had measured and nobody would wear.

**Miss Langton** “He has paid me to say nothing.”
