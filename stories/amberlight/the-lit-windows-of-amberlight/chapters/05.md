## Chapter 5. Every Window Lit

Phoebe Langton came down the staircase of Amberlight House first, and found Mrs Pike in the hall with one hand already on the newel post.

**Mrs Pike** “My dear. The door was standing open to the lane. Where is the chain?”

**Miss Langton** “There is no chain, ma'am. There is a trunk with a handle come away, and a young lady who fainted picking up her letters.”

Harker had come down behind her. He stopped three steps up and spoke over Phoebe's head to both women.

**Mr Harker** “Miss Fairfax was to have been married in June. Her intended's mother said, before nine people, that she had gone after a dead man's money. It was broken off in April, and she came here where no one knew her face.” “You may carry that down the lane with my name at the end of it. A grief the parish knows is easier for her to walk through than a madness it has guessed at.”

**Mrs Hale** “That is a wicked thing for a mother to say.”

**Mrs Pike** “It is, and I never once said madness. Grief, I said. A sorrow is quite another article.”

Mrs Hale pushed her spectacles up into her hair and said nothing. Mrs Pike was looking from Harker on the stair to Phoebe at the foot of it.

**Mrs Pike** “This gentleman settled your account at the draper's and would not put his name to it. That is not meanness, my dear. I shall expect to hear something by Lady Day.”

**Mrs Hale** “I shall repeat it exactly as I had it, ma'am.”

**Miss Langton** “Then repeat the one pound eleven with it, and say it was owed.”

* * *

On Monday morning Augusta Fairfax crossed the yard in black bombazine, cut to her own measure, taken standing up on the Saturday. Mr Rowe held the schoolroom door for her.

**Miss Fairfax** “I should like to hear the youngest read, if you will have me. I am not fit for much else yet.”

The Ludd boy had put his ear to that wall and heard her crying through it. He read her four lines about a ship and got two of them right.

At dusk Phoebe walked down the lane with Harker beside her, because he had said he would see her to the green and she had not troubled to argue it.

**Mr Harker** “I gave you a poplin gown worn through at the elbow and told you to manage. It is still on the chair in my hall. I have not been able to put it anywhere.”

**Miss Langton** “And I stood in the lane counting three guineas into my palm and thought the worst of you. We were both wrong in the same hall, sir, within five minutes.”

At the turn she looked back. The shutters were off the main block. There was a candle in every window of it. The east wing was lit too, where Mr Rowe was locking up.

**Mr Harker** “Send your bill to the house for Miss Fairfax's mourning. Put your name to it, and I shall put mine under it.”
