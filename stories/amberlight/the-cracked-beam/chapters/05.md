## Chapter 5. What Was Seen

The library window looks north up the lane and takes in the corner of the green, and Mrs Hale had been at it since the coach came in. She saw Nye's cart come round with the hurdles still on it. She saw Mr Rowe get down and hand Miss Vane down after him. She saw no maid, no Mrs Pike, and no daylight, and she saw Miss Vane laughing. The whole of it took half a minute. It kept the parish occupied until Tuesday.

By eight the next morning she had told the ostler at the Bell and both the Barrow girls. She told it each time precisely as it had happened. She told Mrs Pike at Mrs Pike's own gate, before nine.

**Mrs Hale** “Six miles out and six miles back and coming in at dusk, and him handing her down in front of the whole Bell yard, and her laughing so she could not get her foot to the step.”

Mrs Pike took hold of her own gate post with both hands.

**Mrs Pike** “There. There. I said Wednesday would answer. I said it at my own table with the cloth still on it, and there was not one of you would believe me.”

**Mrs Hale** “You did say so.”

**Mrs Pike** “A fortnight is all it wanted. And her uncle holds the living, which is the beauty of it.”

Mrs Hale looked up the lane, and then back at her.

**Mrs Hale** “Whose uncle?”

**Mrs Pike** “Miss Marchmont's uncle.”

**Mrs Hale** “It was not Miss Marchmont in the cart. It was Miss Vane.”

Mrs Pike went indoors and shut her own door in Mrs Hale's face, which she had never done to anybody in thirty years. She had sent them off herself.

Before noon she had assured the sexton, Mrs Yardley, and Hollis's housekeeper that it was nothing at all, a wheel and a mason and no more in it. By four o'clock the parish was entirely satisfied that there was a great deal in it.

* * *

Judith went up on the Monday for the black thread she had not got on the Friday. The younger Barrow girl was minding the counter, and lowered her voice, and said it must have been dreadful to be seen crying on the curate's arm, and that Sarah Copp said he had spoken there in the road and been refused.

**Miss Vane** “Where did you have that?” said Judith.

The girl said she had it from her mother, who had it from Mrs Pike, who had said there was nothing in it.

**Miss Vane** “Two reels. And I will take the Cobbe list down off the pin while I am here, since nobody has asked for it.”

She paid, and crossed the green at a pace her brother could hear through the wall of the dispensary. She had been laughed at in this parish before. She had never been pitied for a thing that had not happened.

Owen had it an hour later from a carter's wife who had come in about a cough and stayed for the rest of it. He shut the door behind her and turned the sign round at half past three, which he had not done on a working day since their father died.

**Miss Vane** “Owen.” said Judith, from the passage.

**Mr Vane** “Get your tea.”

**Miss Vane** “It was a wheel. There is nothing to be done about it that will not make it worse.”

Owen took his hat down off the peg.

**Mr Vane** “There is one thing, and it is not yours to do, and I have been standing behind that counter all day listening to it.”

She asked him where he was going.

**Mr Vane** “Up to his lodging. I am going to ask him plainly what he means to do about your name, and I shall want an answer before I come home.”
