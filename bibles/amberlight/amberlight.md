# Middle Watch: Amberlight

A Hampshire parish over the course of a few years, told one household at a time.
The unifying thing is the place: the same green, the same inn, the same Tuesday
coach, seen from a different doorway each story. Somebody who has watched three
of these should recognize the geography before they recognize anybody in it.

The register is domestic and small. Nothing here is a matter of national event.
The stakes are a living, a reputation, an engagement, a debt, a letter — and they
are enormous to the people holding them, because in a parish of four hundred
there is nowhere to go and be someone else.

## What recurs

The Bell and its two coach days. The circulating library above the draper's,
which functions as the parish's real information system. Amberlight House at the
end of the lane, which everyone has an opinion about. The walk to Winchester,
which is eleven miles and therefore a decision rather than an errand.

## What must stay consistent

The distances, the coach days, and the value of the living. Mrs Pike is always
older than Mr Rowe and always knows more than he does.

Ages in `recurring_cast` are as of the summer of 1817, which is the anchor year.
A story set later moves everyone forward together.

Amberlight House is no longer shut. The east wing is a schoolroom from the summer
of 1817, and the rest of the house remains unoccupied — the family did not move
into it and did not sell it. A story may open any part of it, but the east wing
is already spoken for and the estate paid for it.

Judith Vane and Silas Rowe are engaged from June 1817 and marry. Neither marries
anybody else, in any story, at any date. A story set before that summer may use
the estrangement between them; a story set after it may not. She is Miss Vane
before the wedding and Mrs Rowe after it.

The wedding itself is not yet written. *The Cracked Beam* ends with the banns
first read, so the exact date is still open and a later story may set it, but
only between those banns and whenever that story is placed.

## What each story does on its own

Every story stands completely alone. A viewer arrives at any one of them first,
so nothing may depend on having seen another: no recaps, no cliffhangers, no arc
that only pays off elsewhere. Recurring characters get introduced fresh each time
in a line or two, the way a neighbour would be. The series is a shared world, not
a serial.

## What goes in this file

Only what a finished story has established on screen. Not intentions, not plans
for later, not anything from an outline that has not been written and approved.
If a story changes the parish, this file is updated when that story is finished
and the change is recorded in `changelog.md`.

## Voice casting note

Mrs Hale is cast on Charlotte, which is also the narrator voice. That was
workable in *The Cracked Beam*, where she speaks in three short passages well
separated from narration. A story that puts her in sustained dialogue should
recast her — Lily is free whenever Miss Marchmont is not in the story.
