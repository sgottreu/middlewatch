# Middle Watch Fantasy Series Bible (Working Draft)

Working title: Mapping the Broken Edges (alternative: The Broken Edges; stories may be subtitled by region) Status: Early worldbuilding. Structure mirrors the Amberlight bible.

## Premise

About twenty years ago, a Cataclysm reshaped the world. Geological upheaval and other forces cut kingdoms and regions off from one another. The stories are set in the aftermath, as people begin crossing the gaps and documenting what has changed.

## Canon

- A Cataclysm occurred. It happened about 20 years ago, within living memory. Its cause is deliberately kept ambiguous in-world; characters and documenters may hold competing theories.
- Kingdoms and places have been disconnected by geological changes and possibly other causes.
- Travellers move between the isolated regions.
- People are documenting the changed world.

## Open Questions

- None currently open.

## Setting

The world is broken into isolated pockets. Each one can have its own flavour, customs and history since the Cataclysm. That suits the linked-standalone format: every story can open in a new place and still stand on its own.

### Technology

The technology is late medieval, with some precision clockwork. Everyday life is mostly medieval: swords, lanterns, horses, and simple water and wind power. Simple mechanical devices that a local craftsman can build without much manufacturing, such as winches, pulleys, levers and cranks, are common. Precision clockwork and gears are specialist and expensive, used by guilds, wealthy households and the documenters' instruments. Steam power does not exist yet; the world is not there technologically.

### Magic

Magic is latent and subtle, never the main way things get done. It is tied to the Cataclysm, either stirred up by it or left over from it. So the mystery of the Cataclysm and the nature of magic are explored together. No one casts spells. Magic is worked like chemistry and craft: alchemists draw on magically affected materials, and artificers build them into devices and instruments, such as the Glasswood leaf detectors. Tools and weapons can be imbued with magic, but this is rare: magic is still an emerging concept, and few people know how to do it.

### Flora and Fauna

The Cataclysm mutated plants and animals. The changes vary: a few creatures are mildly monstrous, but most are simply odd, familiar things altered in strange ways. Beyond mutations, the latent magic has also brought entirely new creatures into being. It is often unclear whether a creature is a mutated species or a new one, and documenters may disagree. Changed and new species are both part of what the documenters record.

### Travel and Trade

Travel between regions is slow and hard: on foot, by mule or cart along surviving roads, and by boat in the Sunken Reach. Each region has goods others need, such as minerals, leaves, fish and records, but routes are dangerous and uncontrolled. Trade is the main source of tension between regions. Traders hire mappers on contract to chart safe routes, pulling the documenting groups into commerce. This creates a constant tension the mappers must balance: paid contracts versus honest records, such as traders wanting routes kept secret or made to seem safer than they are. Ease of trade varies (starting point): Glasswood and Ashfall trade most easily, matching their partnership; the Sunken Reach is easy by water but hard overland; the Shelf is hardest to reach, via its cliff paths. Barriers beyond geology are mostly regional. The chasm fog of the Riven Plains stretches west toward the border of the Sunken Reach, limiting travel between the two regions. Water flowing out of the Glasswood, the Hollowmere and the Ashfall Marches carries magic sediment and is bad to drink. The streams and rivers run west, staying bad until the magic sediment settles out downstream. The water clears before it reaches Tollgate, so the problem is logistical: travellers in the east must carry enough clean water with them. Nobody has studied where the magic sediment settles: people mostly pass through the area rather than stay, so its possible value is still waiting to be discovered. In the Ashfall Marches, gas from the vents pools in low hollows, creating dead zones of bad air where animals and people can pass out; routes have to skirt them. Across all regions, dangerous wildlife is a general hazard: both changed creatures and ordinary aggressive animals. In the Southern Teeth, tremors and rockfalls around the new peaks can reshape or close the open passes, so their geography shifts over time; after a tremor, the villages keeping the tolled passes have work to do clearing and repairing them.

## Regions (draft, to refine)

- **The Sunken Reach:** a lowland kingdom drowned by the Cataclysm. Its people fled, and new towns grew up in the surrounding hills. Some communities are now building outward from the water's edge, with docks, piers and stilt extensions. Settlements (four or five): Hartsford, an existing pre-Cataclysm hill town near the new shoreline that refugees fled to, now the main town; its refugee camps sprawled downhill to the water and became Shoreside, an attached shoreline settlement building out on docks and stilts; Pell's Rest, a small, remote hill settlement founded after the flood by the Pell family, who ran a large ranching operation before the Cataclysm; they settled there with their employees and workers; and Floatsome, a floating boat town of fishers and salvagers. Floatsome began with old boats that still floated but could no longer work; they were lashed together into platforms, and it has grown into a hodgepodge of hulls, decks and add-ons. The drowned towns themselves stand abandoned, with old records in the streets below. What draws outsiders, including the collectors: the drowned capital, with a rumoured sealed royal vault possibly holding clues about the Cataclysm; and salvaged objects that seem magic-touched, traced to a fissure beneath the water that leaks magic. The fissure likely opened during the Cataclysm, but the generation that lived through it never bothered salvaging; it is only now being noticed as a younger generation, led by Jory Brennick, takes up salvage.
- **The Shelf:** a city lifted onto a plateau when the land split, far too high for lifts or cables. The only links to the world below, if any, are long, winding cliff paths. Some farmland rose with it, but not enough. At first they farmed fields at the foot of the cliffs, but hauling food up the winding paths proved too hard. Out of necessity, the city became a container garden: pots, planters, terraces and rooftop beds on every spare surface. The old lower fields are now largely abandoned. Water comes from rain catchment, cisterns and a spring on the plateau. It is held in common and strictly rationed, and that shared discipline helps make the community tight-knit and wary of outsiders. It is deeply isolated, proud and inward-looking.
- **The Glasswood:** once a major lumber region. The Cataclysm mutated the forest: its leaves now chime in the wind, and locals read the sounds as warnings. The changed wood is useless as timber, so the old lumber towns lost their trade. A new one is emerging: the leaves seem to react to the latent magic, and harvested leaves are being tried as detectors, including by documenters.
- **The Ashfall Marches:** a frontier of cooled lava and steam vents, rich in strange minerals tied to the latent magic, making it a mining region of prospectors, miners and rough camps. Some old towns were buried by lava, and miners sometimes break into their ruins. Others on the edges survived and now serve as supply towns for the camps.
- **The Riven Plains:** once the breadbasket that fed the other regions, now split by deep chasms into a maze of broken fields and makeshift bridges. It is still farmed: when the big fields split, farmers simply worked whatever land was left on their side of the chasm. Several villages survived intact; others were split by chasms, leaving remnant halves on either side. There are usually a couple of makeshift bridges near each surviving village, since villages have the people to build them; out among the farms there may be one or two, built where good farmland lies on both sides and a group of farmers is willing to cross to work it, or simply so a farmer can cross to help a neighbour on the other side. The Plains are also the gateway to the Stillwinter: travellers heading north pass through them, which is the main reason outsiders cross the region. Weird things happen down in the chasms between the fields. A fog pools at the bottom that does not behave like normal fog: it never burns off, drifts against the wind, and on some nights creeps over the edges onto the fields, so farmers keep their animals in. It may be the Stillwinter's slowed time seeping south. That influence also reaches the northern fields and affects the crops there (possibly slow to grow, slow to rot).
- **The Hollowmere:** a marsh whose water glows faintly at night. Before the Cataclysm it was patches of grazing land beside ordinary wetlands; the land sank, and runoff from the Glasswood and Ashfall Marches turned it all into deep marsh, drowning old farmsteads. The half-sunk farmhouses are points of interest for future stories. Their upper floors, above the water, may be nests for marsh creatures, changed or new, or camps used by alchemists gathering reagents. Documenters and mappers may find remnants of people who stayed there before. The former herders moved to the edges; on the strip of land between the Glasswood and the marsh, a few still graze cattle, including in the open woodland at the Glasswood's thinner edge. The cattle drink marsh water and are slowly changing because of it. Alchemists come for its reagents; locals are wary of anything that grows there.
- **Tollgate:** a surviving market town at a natural crossroads where traders and mappers meet. It was already a trading town before the Cataclysm, and its importance grew after it, as one of the few places routes still connect. The name dates from the town's founding, long before the Cataclysm, when tolls were charged at its narrow entrances. As the town grew the tolls became expensive enough to drive traders away, so they were abolished; only the name remains; the centre of clockwork craft, since its position at the crossroads makes it the natural place to make and distribute precision work across the regions.
- **The Stillwinter:** a cold northern stretch locked in permanent late autumn: always cold, grey and still, where nothing quite dies or grows and crops ripen but never rot. The stuck season is the visible sign that time itself runs slow there, on a gradient. At the edges it is only slightly slow; deeper north everything grows stiller, until near the heart nothing has decayed since the Cataclysm and travellers return having lost more days than they thought. Only 20 years in, the effect on people is modest: no one is near-immortal, but long-time residents have aged a little less than they should have. Quiet and eerie.
- **The Southern Teeth:** a south-central mountain range that is both a barrier and a region. Most of the range predates the Cataclysm, but some new peaks thrust up, and one or two old ones collapsed, opening new passes. Hardy mountain villages survived the Cataclysm almost untouched, so their people remember the old world best. They guard the old passes and charge tolls to cross. The new passes are open and unclaimed, free but unguarded and riskier, so many travellers choose the tolled passes because the tolls pay for security.

Stillwinter at the far north, with the Shelf just south of it. In the east, the Glasswood (north-east) and the Ashfall Marches (south-east), with the Hollowmere in the low-lying land between them. Runoff from both regions drains into the marsh, which may explain its glow and magical properties. Sunken Reach in the south-west lowlands. The Southern Teeth, a mountain range, run across the south-central area, between the Sunken Reach and the Ashfall Marches, south of the Riven Plains.

## The Documenters

### Regional Documenting Groups

There is no single central institution. After the Cataclysm, organised groups formed separately in different regions to survey, map and record the changed world. Over time they have slowly begun working together, sharing records and coordinating, though each keeps its own local character. Cooperation is uneven: some groups work closely together, while others remain more apart. The Shelf's group is the most isolated, cut off by the plateau and its people's inward-looking nature.

Regional focus. The groups are still young, about 20 years old, so they likely go by plain, generic names. Each region has its own emphasis:

- **Sunken Reach:** no formal group. The shoreline barely changed after the flood, and paper records did not survive 20 years underwater. Instead, a couple of salvagers keep track of what they have searched, and one or two people from Floatsome chart debris and flooded homes near the surface that could tangle boats.
- **Glasswood:** naturalists and alchemical researchers, studying the changed flora and fauna and the physical and magical properties of the trees and leaves.
- **Ashfall Marches:** a mineral survey, cataloguing ores and the magic-tied minerals.
- **The Shelf:** an archive, keeping records and histories of the world before.

The Glasswood and Ashfall groups are the closest partners, comparing magic-touched plant materials with magic-tied minerals. The Hollowmere has no group of its own; the two groups study it jointly, since it is fed by runoff from both. People are noticing how closely the three regions are tied by the magic, and there are early inklings of a merged group covering all three.

Regions without a formal group:

- **The Riven Plains:** none. It is agricultural; its farmers know their land and simply deal with it.
- **Tollgate:** no group of its own, but it is where the regional groups meet.
- **The Stillwinter:** none. Stories are told about it, and some people from Tollgate want to go and investigate.
- **The Southern Teeth:** no formal group. Village elders and keepers hold informal records, including memories of the old world.

**The first collectors:** with records scattered across formal groups, informal keepers and individuals, one or two people have decided it all needs organising. They have set out to travel the regions and gather everyone's data into one body of knowledge, the earliest seed of a shared effort. Traders are the catalyst: their talk of distant regions sparks the idea, and someone in Tollgate decides to become the collectors' patron and fund the work.

Group names (plain and practical, as befits young groups; colourful nicknames are rare for now):

- **Sunken Reach:** no formal group (informal salvager and Floatsome records)
- **The Shelf:** the Shelf Archive
- **Glasswood:** the Glasswood Society
- **Ashfall Marches:** the Ashfall Mineral Survey

### Independent Chroniclers

For now, the independent chroniclers are simply traders and the stories they carry between regions: informal, word-of-mouth accounts rather than written records. They may record things the regional groups would rather leave out. The groups and the independents are not rival factions. They are two kinds of documenter working the same world.

## Story Ideas

- **The Glasswood leaves:** a documenter investigates whether the leaves or trees can truly be used, as magic detectors, in potions, or as spell components, while a struggling former lumber town hopes the answer is yes.
- **Into the Stillwinter:** drawn by stories of a place where time runs slow, a party sets out from Tollgate to investigate whether the rumours are true.
- **The settled sediment:** on the way east, Tobias and Tess notice where the magic sediment settles out of the westward rivers, a stretch nobody has studied because people only pass through, and begin to suspect it is worth something.
- **Sunken Reach seeds (parked):** Hartsford and Shoreside disputing who owns salvage and the drowned land; Floatsome navigating by the spires and towers still above the water; salvagers finding a sealed building still dry inside after 20 years.

## Recurring Cast

Structure: a rotating cast. Each region has its own local mappers, matching the decentralised groups. The first collectors (see The Documenters) are the linking characters who recur between stories. No other recurring traders or wanderers for now; more may be added later. Defined so far: Garret Marsh, Wren Calloway, Maud and Jory Brennick, Bram Tolliver, Tobias Finch, Tess Ridley and Matthias Kettering. Voice casting is on hold; it will be done in the review UI's Casting page.

### Garret Marsh (Glasswood mapper)

A middle-aged man, formerly a lumberjack, now mapping for the Glasswood group. He remembers the forest before the Cataclysm. He is driven by genuine curiosity about the changes as well as a need to prove the leaves are valuable and save his town.

### Wren Calloway (Shelf archivist)

A young woman, born just after the Cataclysm, who keeps the Shelf's archive. She has never known the old world except through the records she guards. Sharp and proud like her city, but quietly restless and curious about what lies down the cliff paths. Her restlessness pulls against her region's isolation.

### Maud Brennick (Floatsome hazard-charter)

An older woman in her forties from Floatsome, practical rather than sentimental. She grew up in one of the drowned towns and knows its old streets, which makes her the best at charting debris and flooded homes near the surface. A snagged hull can sink a family's living, so Floatsome pays her in fish and favours to keep the channels safe. She started out fishing, then began helping keep the hazard records. Later her son, Jory Brennick, caught the salvaging bug and drew her into it, so she now salvages too. Jory, young and eager to go deeper than Maud would like, is the one whose salvage first turns up magic-touched objects, leading to the fissure beneath the water.

### Bram Tolliver (Ashfall miner-surveyor)

A younger man in his late twenties, a miner with a gift for reading rock. He began noting which seams held the strange minerals, and the Ashfall Mineral Survey grew out of his notebooks. Following the seams, he also noticed oddities in the neighbouring Hollowmere. Rough around the edges, he is more at home underground than at a meeting in Tollgate.

### The collectors: Tobias Finch and Tess Ridley

A pair who travel the regions gathering records, funded by a Tollgate patron. One is a meticulous record-keeper who wants everything catalogued properly; the other is a well-travelled former trader's hand who knows the routes and the people. Method and road-sense together. The record-keeper, Tobias Finch, a man in his mid-twenties, is the patron's own bookkeeper in Tollgate, who out of curiosity copied traders' stories and rough sketch maps into personal notebooks. The patron noticed the notebooks and saw someone already doing the job unpaid. His partner, Tess Ridley, is a woman around thirty, a seasoned trader. After a couple of bad runs or an accident (deliberately left open to explore in future stories), she wants to keep travelling but is tired of the hassle of trading, and of the criminals and dangers that come with it; collecting lets her stay on the road without the cargo. The patron has traded with her before. Patron and trader see the collecting venture as a source of more profit: better knowledge of routes and regions means better trade. It starts as a profit venture; the patron is not solely focused on profit, but when profit and honest record-keeping conflict, profit wins. This creates an occasional tension between the bookkeeper and the patron: not constant, but one that can surface in different stories.

### Matthias Kettering (Tollgate patron)

A man in his fifties, a clockwork merchant who also makes the clockwork, selling instruments from Tollgate, including those the mappers rely on. A big reason for funding the collectors: stories from traders about the magic-touched leaves of the Glasswood and minerals of the Ashfall Marches, and what they might mean for clockwork. Employs Tobias Finch and has traded with Tess Ridley.
