# Middle Watch: Amberlight

A Hampshire parish over the course of a few years, told one household at a time.
The unifying thing is the place: the same green, the same inn, the same Tuesday
coach, seen from a different doorway each story. Somebody who has watched three
of these should recognize the geography before they recognize anybody in it.

The register is domestic and small. Nothing here is a matter of national event.
The stakes are a living, a reputation, an engagement, a debt, a letter — and they
are enormous to the people holding them, because in a parish of four hundred
there is nowhere to go and be someone else.

## What recurs

The Bell and its two coach days. The circulating library above the draper's,
which functions as the parish's real information system. Amberlight House
standing empty at the end of the lane, which everyone has an opinion about. The
walk to Winchester, which is eleven miles and therefore a decision rather than an
errand.

## What must stay consistent

The distances, the coach days, and the value of the living. Mrs Pike is always
older than Mr Rowe and always knows more than he does. Amberlight House stays
empty unless a story is specifically about it being occupied — and if one is,
that becomes canon and this file gets updated.

## What each story does on its own

Every story stands completely alone. A viewer arrives at any one of them first,
so nothing may depend on having seen another: no recaps, no cliffhangers, no arc
that only pays off elsewhere. Recurring characters get introduced fresh each time
in a line or two, the way a neighbour would be. The series is a shared world, not
a serial.
