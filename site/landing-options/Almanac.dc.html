WWhen the house is abed and the candle burns low, there is yet a little company to be kept. Herein the Proprietor offers two kinds of it: tales told aloud in a plain and steady voice, and long sounds of weather and water to sit beside you until the morning bell. Nothing is asked of the Reader but to listen, or to sleep, as the hour requires.

No. I.

Stories

being Narrated Fiction, read aloud

Original short tales, set down with a care for history and spoken by lamplight. Many wear the manners of the Regency: letters sealed with wax, roads bad in winter, secrets kept below stairs. Each may be heard entire in a single sitting.

No. II.

Ambience

being Soundscapes for the Small Hours

Long, unhurried airs of weather and hearth, made to be left playing while one reads, works, or drifts off. No tune to follow and no voice to attend; only the room, the window, and whatever is falling past it.

Wherein will be found
  • ‣Candlelight, snow, rain upon the glass, and the sea at night.
  • ‣Some drawn from the very places where the Stories are set.
  • ‣Others that keep no company but their own.

Notice to the Reader

touching the Name of this Publication

Aboard ship the night is divided into watches, and the middle watch is that kept from midnight until four in the morning. It is the quietest of them: the hands below are asleep, the deck is dark, and one person stands awake to keep the lamp lit and the course true.

These Stories and Soundscapes are made for that hour, and for anyone who finds themselves keeping it, by choice or otherwise.

Correspondence may be directed to hello@middlewatch.co

Letters are read by the Proprietor himself, generally in the small hours, and answered as the watch allows.
